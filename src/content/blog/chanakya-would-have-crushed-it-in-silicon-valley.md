> **🖼️ Cover image:** A public-domain map or painting of the Maurya Empire from Wikimedia Commons (search "Maurya Empire map" or "Chanakya"), or a split frame: an old palm-leaf manuscript on one side, a server rack on the other.

Peter Thiel wrote an essay in 2014 called ["Competition Is for Losers"](https://www.wsj.com/articles/peter-thiel-competition-is-for-losers-1410535536), and Silicon Valley has been treating it like scripture ever since. Build a monopoly, own your corner, never fight a fair fight. Very edgy. Very contrarian.

Somewhere in the 4th century BCE, a Brahmin named Chanakya was already running the advanced version. He took a young nobody named Chandragupta, toppled the [Nanda dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Empire), helped build the Maurya Empire, and then wrote the operating manual for running it: the [Arthashastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthashastra). Treasury management, spy networks, alliance math, what to do about a minister skimming off the top. It's basically a Y Combinator handbook, if YC also taught you how to poison a rival king.

In my [last post on this](/post/outgrowing-idealisms), I dropped Chanakya's line "dharmasya mūlam arthah": material prosperity is the root of righteousness. Then I left it there. But after the last two months in tech, I can't stop seeing the Arthashastra everywhere. So here's the experiment: four lessons from a 2,300-year-old book, and the companies currently living them out, whether they know it or not.

All the Kautilya quotes below come from R. Shamasastry's [1915 English translation](https://archive.org/stream/kautilyasarthash00sham/kautilyasarthash00sham_djvu.txt), which is public domain. (Kautilya and Chanakya are the same guy. He had more aliases than a crypto founder.)

## I. The Treasury

> "Kautilya holds that wealth and wealth alone is important, inasmuch as charity and desire depend upon wealth for their realisation."
>
> "ALL undertakings depend upon finance. Hence foremost attention shall be paid to the treasury."

Most ancient philosophy treats money like an embarrassing cousin. Chanakya puts it at the top of the org chart. Charity, pleasure, war: all of it runs on the treasury. No treasury, no army. No army, no kingdom.

Now look at what the AI kings are doing. Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft and Meta are on track to spend roughly [\$725 billion on capex in 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/magnificent-7-earnings-rush-reveals-ai-spending-surge-with-hyperscaler-capex-set-to-reach-725-billion-in-2026-224901707.html). That is a treasury being converted into an army in real time, except the soldiers are GPUs and the forts are data centers in places most people couldn't find on a map.

Chanakya would approve of the instinct. He'd also check the books. On November 20, the S&P 500 fell 3.7% and the Nasdaq [wiped out its entire year](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/20/investing/us-stocks-anthropic-ai), as investors started asking whether all that spending ever comes back. As Baird's Michael Antonelli [put it](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/national-international/november-20-stock-market-updates/4179477/), "The whole notion that AI can continue to spend unlimited amounts of money and support the stock market, I think the notion of that is ending as we speak." A treasury that funds an army is strategy. A treasury that funds an army that hasn't won anything yet is just a very expensive parade.

> **✍️ Your take:** Is the AI capex race a Chanakya move (build the army first, the empire follows) or a king emptying the treasury on a war he can't finish?
>
> _Starter take:_ Chanakya never said spend everything. He said the treasury comes first. The winners will be the ones who still have money left when the music stops, not the ones who built the biggest fort.

## II. The Circle

> "The king who is situated anywhere immediately on the circumference of the conqueror's territory is termed the enemy."
>
> "The king who is likewise situated close to the enemy, but separated from the conqueror only by the enemy, is termed the friend (of the conqueror)."

This is the mandala theory, and it's the most quietly brilliant idea in the book. Your neighbour is your natural enemy. Your neighbour's neighbour is your natural friend. Draw the circles outward and you can predict most of the alliances on the map before anyone signs anything.

Swap "territory" for "market" and it still works. Companies sitting right next to each other in the stack are rivals. The ones a ring further out become allies against them. Nvidia, which sells the shovels, has been busy making friends one ring out: it was preparing to put [about \$10 billion into Anthropic](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/why-did-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resign-and-who-is-colette-kress) around its IPO, and it agreed to acquire Hugging Face. Meanwhile the big clouds keep designing their own chips, which in Kautilya's terms means the friend is slowly turning into the neighbour. And you already know what the neighbour becomes.

The same geometry explains why the model labs and the clouds keep getting weirdly cozy and then weirdly frosty. Nobody is being emotional. They're just reading the map.

## III. The Spies

> "The arrow shot by an archer may or may not kill a single man; but skilful intrigue devised by wise men can kill even those who are in the womb."

Book I of the Arthashastra has an entire chapter called "The Institution of Spies." Chanakya wanted agents disguised as students, ascetics, householders and merchants, watching ministers and rival courts alike. Elsewhere he spends pages ranking a king's vices, with addiction to women weighed right alongside gambling, because a king with a weakness is a kingdom with a back door.

Which brings us to the most Arthashastra story of the year. On November 12, [Jensen Huang resigned](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-live-updates.html) as CEO of Nvidia after 33 years. Nvidia controls an estimated 90% of the market for advanced AI chips. In the days before, Huang had admitted to being unfaithful to his wife, the board had opened an independent investigation into [reports he'd been targeted by Chinese intelligence](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/why-did-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resign-and-who-is-colette-kress), and new allegations surfaced about a relationship with a senior executive who reported to him. All of this from the guy who spent years lobbying against restrictions on selling Nvidia chips to China.

To be fair to Jensen, Al Jazeera's reporting is clear: "There is no established evidence he knowingly assisted Beijing or that company secrets were compromised." But that's exactly Chanakya's point. The intrigue doesn't have to succeed. It just has to exist. One alleged spy operation took out the most powerful man in the most important supply chain on Earth, and nobody had to prove a thing.

Lloyd Blankfein [summed it up on X](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/why-did-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resign-and-who-is-colette-kress): "Sad about Jensen. Shakespearean." Then he quoted de Gaulle: "The graveyards are filled with indispensable men."

> **✍️ Your take:** Should the CEO of a company this strategically important be held to a statesman's standard, private life included?
>
> _Starter take:_ Yes, and Chanakya would say it isn't even about morality. When your chips are a matter of national security, your personal life is a matter of national security too. That's the price of the crown.

## IV. The Kingmakers

> "Sovereignty (rājatva) is possible only with assistance. A single wheel can never move. Hence he shall employ ministers and hear their opinion."

Silicon Valley loves a founder-king. The visionary who owns the vision, the board, the supervoting shares and the narrative. Jensen in the leather jacket. It's a great story right up until the king disappears, and then everyone finds out whether there was ever a second wheel.

Nvidia is finding out live. CFO [Colette Kress](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/why-did-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resign-and-who-is-colette-kress) is interim CEO, the board is running a search, and TBPN spent the week asking what happens next, including a whole clip of Travis Kalanick on [what he'd do if he were Jensen](https://feeds.transistor.fm/technology-brother). On November 20, Nvidia closed [13.8% lower](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/national-international/november-20-stock-market-updates/4179477/), and it's down more than 25% since the scandal broke. The market is pricing in exactly what Chanakya warned about: a kingdom built on one wheel.

The honest counterpoint is that the single-wheel model is also why these companies win. A committee doesn't bet a graphics company on CUDA. A founder does. And Chanakya himself was a kingmaker, not a king; the system he designed still put Chandragupta at the centre. The trick is a strong king with ministers who could actually run the place if he vanished tomorrow. Most of Silicon Valley has the first part down and is hoping it never needs the second.

> **✍️ Your take:** Founder-king or strong ministers: which kind of company would you rather bet your career on?
>
> _Starter take:_ Founder-king for the first ten years, ministers for the next fifty. The best founders build the second wheel before anyone asks them to.

## Where Chanakya Would Disagree

It'd be easy to read all this and decide Chanakya was just Machiavelli with better weather. He wasn't. Here's the line people tend to skip:

> "In the happiness of his subjects lies his happiness; in their welfare his welfare; whatever pleases himself he shall not consider as good, but whatever pleases his subjects he shall consider as good."

For all the spies and poison and alliance maths, the Arthashastra's scoreboard is the welfare of the people. And right now the subjects are restless. In the recent midterms, candidates pushing to [slow down data center construction](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/national-international/november-20-stock-market-updates/4179477/) won at both the federal and state level. And as I wrote back in April, [San Francisco's housing market](/post/the-san-francisco-housing-market-has-officially-lost-the-plot) lost the plot the moment AI money showed up. The kings are winning the treasury game and losing the welfare one.

Chanakya would tell them what he and Marx told me last time. Like, no. Fix the economy first. For everyone, not just the court.

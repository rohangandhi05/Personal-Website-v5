You're checking out online. A Peloton, a couch, a flight. Below the price: "or 4 interest-free payments of \$X with Affirm."

That's buy now, pay later. And the obvious question: if it's interest-free, how does Affirm make any money?

**Mostly from the store, not you.** Merchants pay Affirm a fee, often a few percent of the sale, to offer it. Why? Because people buy more, and bigger, when they can split it up. The store is basically paying Affirm to make you feel richer.

**Interest, on the bigger loans.** Longer plans, like 12 months on a couch, often do charge interest.

**Selling the loans.** Affirm packages many of the loans and sells them to investors, so it isn't stuck holding all the risk.

**The Affirm Card.** A debit card that lets you split purchases after the fact. It's been growing fast.

The part most people don't know: Affirm was founded in 2012 by Max Levchin, one of the co-founders of PayPal. He's a member of the "PayPal Mafia" along with Elon Musk, Peter Thiel and Reid Hoffman. After building the internet's first big payment system, he decided to rebuild credit.

Its biggest partners are huge: Affirm extended its U.S. deal with Amazon through January 2031, and it powers Shop Pay Installments for Shopify stores. After years of losses, it's now profitable, with an \$80.7 million profit in one quarter last fall and a goal of \$100 billion in yearly purchases.

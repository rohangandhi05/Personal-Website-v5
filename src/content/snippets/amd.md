The two most important people in AI chips are related.

AMD CEO Lisa Su and Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang are distant cousins, both born in Tainan, Taiwan. Thanksgiving must be tense.

When Su took over AMD in 2014, the company was a punchline. The stock was trading for a couple of dollars, and Intel and Nvidia were eating its lunch, dinner and dessert. Her turnaround is one of the great comebacks in tech: first by winning back the PC and server market with Ryzen and EPYC chips, now by going after Nvidia in AI.

How it makes money: selling chips. Processors for laptops, desktops and servers, graphics cards, and AI accelerators called Instinct. The thing most people don't know: AMD's custom chips are inside the PlayStation 5 and the Xbox Series X. Every console war since 2013 has been won by AMD, because AMD was supplying both sides.

The big 2026 story is two blockbuster deals. OpenAI agreed to deploy 6 gigawatts of AMD's AI chips, starting with the MI450 in the second half of this year. Meta signed its own 6-gigawatt deal. And in both cases AMD sweetened it with a warrant that lets the customer earn up to 160 million AMD shares as milestones are hit. Translation: buy enough of our chips and you get a piece of the company. It's a loyalty card, but the points are equity.

Data center revenue hit \$16.6 billion in 2025 and is expected to grow fast as those deals ship.

Public (NASDAQ: AMD). The cousin who was written off is back at the kids' table, asking for the turkey.

Count the letters between the "a" and the "z" in "Andreessen Horowitz."

Sixteen. A-16-Z. That's the whole nickname. Silicon Valley has a gift for naming things.

The firm was founded in 2009 by two people with legendary résumés. Marc Andreessen co-created Mosaic, one of the first web browsers, and co-founded Netscape as a twenty-something. Ben Horowitz ran a company through near-death and wrote a cult-classic book about it, The Hard Thing About Hard Things.

When they started, venture capital firms were mostly a handful of partners writing checks. a16z built itself more like a Hollywood talent agency, with full teams for recruiting, marketing and connecting founders with big customers, a model inspired by the talent agency CAA. It also built a media operation, publishing podcasts, newsletters and essays like Andreessen's famous "Software Is Eating the World."

How it makes money, the classic VC model:
1. **Management fees:** a percentage of the money it manages, paid every year.
2. **Carry:** a share of the profits when investments succeed, typically 20% or more.

Some famous bets: early Facebook, Airbnb, Coinbase, GitHub, Databricks and Anduril. It also has a separate "American Dynamism" fund investing in defense, manufacturing and infrastructure.

The scale is now enormous. In January 2026 it raised over \$15 billion in new funds, which it said was about 18% of all U.S. venture capital raised the year before.

Private. Software ate the world. a16z is trying to own a piece of every bite.

Here's a chain of events most people have never thought about.

1. Your grandma buys an annuity, a retirement product that promises her steady payments for life.
2. She buys it from an insurance company called Athene.
3. Athene is owned by Apollo, one of the biggest investment firms in the world.
4. Apollo takes grandma's premiums and lends that money out, to companies, real estate projects and, lately, AI infrastructure.
5. The loans pay more interest than Athene owes grandma. Apollo keeps the difference.

Congratulations. You now understand the business model that turned Apollo into a firm with about \$1 trillion under management.

Apollo was founded in 1990 by Leon Black, Marc Rowan and Josh Harris, all alumni of Drexel Burnham Lambert, the junk bond firm from the 1980s that inspired Gordon Gekko. It started as a private equity firm, buying companies with borrowed money. But its real superpower today is **private credit**: loans made directly by investment firms instead of banks. As of mid-2026, its credit business alone held about \$849 billion.

How it makes money: management and performance fees from its funds, plus the "spread" from Athene, the gap between what the investments earn and what the insurance policies pay out.

Leon Black stepped down as CEO in 2021 after his financial ties to Jeffrey Epstein came to light. Marc Rowan has run it since and is one of the loudest voices arguing that private credit will replace a big chunk of bank lending.

Public (NYSE: APO). Grandma's an AI investor now. She doesn't know it, but she is.

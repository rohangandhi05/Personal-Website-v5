Everyone knows Apple sells iPhones. Fewer people know Apple is also a landlord, and you live in its building.

The building is your phone. Once you're inside, Apple collects rent:

**The App Store.** A cut of up to 30% on many purchases made through apps. Your kid's V-Bucks? Apple got a slice.

**Google.** Google pays Apple to be the default search engine in Safari. In the Google antitrust trial it came out that the payment was about \$20 billion in 2022 alone. Google pays rent to be the front door.

**Apple Pay.** When you tap your phone at checkout, the bank that issued your card pays Apple a small fee. You pay nothing. Apple still gets paid.

**Subscriptions.** iCloud storage (the "storage full" notification is a business model), Music, TV+, Arcade, AppleCare.

Together that's Services, and it's growing fast: a record \$30.9 billion in the March 2026 quarter alone. Services have much fatter margins than hardware, because selling iCloud space costs Apple almost nothing per gig.

Another thing most people don't realize: Apple designs its own chips now. The M-series chips in Macs and the A-series in iPhones are Apple's own designs, manufactured by TSMC.

And this year, a changing of the guard. Tim Cook, who took over from Steve Jobs in 2011, handed the CEO job to hardware chief John Ternus and became executive chairman.

Public (NASDAQ: AAPL). Rent is due on the first of every month. Your phone will remind you.

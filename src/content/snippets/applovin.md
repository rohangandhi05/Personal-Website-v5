You finish a level in some free phone game. A 30-second ad plays for a different free phone game. You download it. You finish a level. Another ad plays.

There's a very good chance AppLovin was behind both of those ads, and got paid each time.

AppLovin is the most profitable company most people have never heard of. It runs a giant ad marketplace inside mobile apps, powered by its AI engine called Axon. Axon decides which ad to show you, at which moment, to make you most likely to install, click or buy.

How it makes money: advertisers, mostly game developers, pay AppLovin to get new users. AppLovin takes a cut of every ad dollar that flows through its network. And it is ridiculously efficient. In the last quarter of 2025, revenue was \$1.66 billion, up 66%, and it expected about 84% adjusted EBITDA margins for early 2026. That's roughly 84 cents of every dollar turning into operating profit. Most companies dream of 30.

The plot twist people missed: AppLovin used to make games itself. In 2025 it sold its apps business to focus entirely on ads. Now it's pushing beyond games into ads for online shopping brands.

It was added to the S&P 500 in 2025 after a monster run. Then 2026 got rough, as investors started worrying that AI could disrupt ad tech itself, and the stock fell hard.

Founded in 2012 by CEO Adam Foroughi.

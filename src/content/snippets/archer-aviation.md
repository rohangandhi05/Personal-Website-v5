The flying car is real. It's called Midnight. It's just waiting on the FAA.

Archer builds an electric air taxi: a quiet aircraft with 12 propellers that takes off and lands vertically like a helicopter, then flies like a plane. A pilot plus four passengers. The pitch: hop from downtown to the airport in about 10 minutes instead of an hour in traffic.

How it makes money: right now, barely at all. That's normal for a company building an aircraft. The plan is to:
1. Run its own air taxi service and charge per ride, aiming for something close to an Uber Black price.
2. Sell aircraft to airlines and operators. United Airlines placed an order back in 2021.
3. Defense. Archer partnered with Anduril on a hybrid version for the military.

Stellantis, the carmaker behind Jeep and Chrysler, helps it manufacture.

Where things stand: Midnight doesn't have full FAA certification yet. Archer has finished more of the FAA's process than any other air taxi maker, and it's pushing to launch first in the UAE, where regulators created a faster path. The big date on the calendar: Archer is the official air taxi provider for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, flying athletes, fans and VIPs over LA traffic.

Bonus lore: co-founder Brett Adcock left to start Figure AI, the humanoid robot company. One guy, two of the most sci-fi startups in America.

Public (NYSE: ACHR). The Jetsons were set in 2062. Archer is trying to beat that by about 34 years.

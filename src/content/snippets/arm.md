You've used an Arm design today. Probably a few dozen times.

Your phone's processor, Android or iPhone, is almost certainly built on Arm's designs. So are many smartwatches, car systems, smart TVs and, increasingly, laptops and data center servers.

But for most of its history Arm didn't make a single chip. It's an architect, not a builder. Arm designs the blueprints for processors and licenses them to companies like Apple, Qualcomm and Nvidia, who build their own chips from them.

How it makes money, two ways:
1. **Licenses:** an upfront fee to use the blueprints.
2. **Royalties:** a small cut of every chip shipped with its design inside, forever.

Royalties are the beautiful part. Arm does the work once and gets paid on billions of chips for years. In the fiscal year ending March 2026, royalties brought in about \$2.6 billion of its \$4.9 billion revenue.

Fun fact most people don't know: Arm was founded in 1990 as a joint venture between Acorn Computers, VLSI Technology and Apple. Apple co-founded the company whose designs now run the iPhone.

Arm was bought by SoftBank in 2016, went public again in 2023, and SoftBank still owns most of it.

The 2026 plot twist: Arm started selling its own chip, the Arm AGI CPU, for data centers, with partners including Meta and OpenAI. The architect has picked up a hammer, which makes some of its own customers a little nervous.

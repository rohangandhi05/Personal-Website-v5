Bill Shufelt was a trader at a hedge fund who decided to stop drinking. He still wanted to have a beer with friends after a run or at a barbecue. But non-alcoholic beer in 2017 meant a dusty six-pack of O'Doul's in the back of the fridge that tasted like disappointment.

So he quit finance and started Athletic Brewing in Connecticut with brewer John Walker. Their goal: non-alcoholic beer that actually tastes like good craft beer.

It worked so well that Athletic is now the No. 1 non-alcoholic beer brand in the U.S., with about 18% of the entire non-alcoholic beer category in early 2026. That's not a niche of craft beer. That's beating giant beer companies' own non-alcoholic versions.

How it makes money: selling beer. Cans in grocery stores and liquor stores, on tap at bars and stadiums, and direct online. The twist is the timing: younger people are drinking less alcohol than any generation before them, and "Dry January" turned into a year-round lifestyle for a lot of people. Athletic rode that wave right into the middle of the beer aisle.

The thing most people don't realize: non-alcoholic beer is technically hard to make well. Most big brewers make regular beer and then strip the alcohol out, which can wreck the flavor. Athletic built its own brewing process to make it non-alcoholic from the start.

The company was valued at about \$800 million in 2024 and expects to brew more than 500,000 barrels in 2026. Shufelt predicts non-alcoholic will be over 20% of the beer market within five years.

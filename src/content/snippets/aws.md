You think of Amazon as a store. Amazon's accountants think of the store as the thing the cloud pays for.

Amazon Web Services rents out computing power: servers, storage, databases and now AI chips, by the hour, to basically every company on the internet. When Netflix streams, when a startup launches, when a bank runs its apps, there's a good chance the bill goes to AWS.

The math is wild. In the April–June 2026 quarter, AWS made \$16.6 billion in operating profit, out of \$27.5 billion for all of Amazon. That's about 60% of Amazon's profit from a business most shoppers have never heard of.

It started almost by accident. Amazon had built huge infrastructure to survive holiday shopping spikes, and in 2006 it began renting that capability to others. Twenty years later, AWS is running at about \$184 billion a year, and grew 39% in the July–September quarter, its fastest in nearly five years.

The part people don't expect: Amazon designs its own chips. Graviton for regular computing and Trainium for AI training, so customers don't have to rely entirely on Nvidia. It's also a key partner for Anthropic, which has committed at least \$110 billion to AWS.

Andy Jassy, who built AWS, now runs all of Amazon. AWS is run by Matt Garman.

Public, as part of Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN). Your two-day shipping is great. The real Prime is the cloud.

February 2021. A brutal winter storm hits Texas, the power grid nearly collapses, and millions of people lose electricity for days in freezing temperatures.

Base Power is built around a simple idea: what if every house had its own big battery, and what if all those batteries worked together?

Base was founded in Austin in 2023 by Zach Dell (yes, Michael Dell's son) and Justin Lopas. It installs a large battery outside your home, about 39 kilowatt-hours in its newest model, which is enough to keep a typical house running through an outage.

How it makes money, and this is the clever part:

1. **It becomes your electricity company.** In Texas you can choose your power provider, so you switch to Base and pay your power bill to them.
2. **You lease the battery** for a small fee instead of paying thousands upfront.
3. **It runs all the batteries as one giant virtual power plant.** When electricity is cheap, the batteries charge. When the grid is stressed and prices spike, like a 105-degree August afternoon, Base sells that stored power back to the grid at high prices.

So your house gets backup power, and Base gets paid for being a tiny piece of a massive, distributed power plant. It also partners with utilities that want extra grid capacity without building new plants.

It has installed batteries at about 17,000 homes, mostly in Texas, and builds them in its own Austin factory.

In August 2026 it raised \$1 billion at a \$13 billion valuation, up from \$4 billion less than a year earlier.

Private. Turns out Dell's next-generation hardware company is a battery.

Rent is the biggest bill most young people pay. And for decades, you couldn't earn anything for paying it. Landlords don't take credit cards, or charge a 3% fee if they do.

Bilt changed that. Founded in 2021 by Ankur Jain, it lets you pay rent with its card and earn points, with no fee. Those points transfer to airlines and hotels, which made Bilt the darling of every points-and-miles nerd on the internet. Paying rent became the most rewarding thing many people did all month.

So who's paying for those free points? This is the fun part.

For years, a big chunk of the cost was absorbed by **Wells Fargo**, which issued the original Bilt card. Rent payments earn the bank almost nothing in fees, but Bilt cardholders still racked up points. Reports suggested Wells Fargo was losing as much as \$10 million a month on the partnership. In early 2026 Wells Fargo exited, and Bilt moved to new cards issued through a fintech called Cardless, with an annual fee on its premium cards.

How Bilt makes money, beyond the card:
1. **Landlords and property managers** pay Bilt to be part of its network, because it keeps tenants happy and paying on time.
2. **Neighborhood merchants,** like restaurants and gyms, pay to reach Bilt members near their homes.
3. **Card economics** from the new card lineup.

The bigger vision: own the whole housing wallet, from rent to, now, mortgages and home purchases.

It was valued at \$10.75 billion in 2025.

Private. The landlord still gets paid. Now you do too.

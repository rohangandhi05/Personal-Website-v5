You've used Booking Holdings. You just didn't know it was all the same company.

It owns:
- **Booking.com**, the giant
- **Priceline**, the one with William Shatner
- **Kayak**, the flight search site
- **Agoda**, huge in Asia
- **OpenTable**, for restaurant reservations
- **Rentalcars.com**

Comparison shopping on Kayak, then booking on Booking.com, then eating dinner reserved on OpenTable? Congratulations, you've been in one company's funnel all night.

It started as Priceline in 1998, with a "name your own price" gimmick and ads starring William Shatner, Captain Kirk himself. Priceline nearly died in the dot-com crash. Then it bought a small Dutch hotel booking site in 2005, Booking.com, which turned into the main event. The company eventually renamed itself after the thing it bought.

How it makes money: mostly commissions. When you book a hotel or vacation rental, the property pays Booking a cut, often around 15%. Hotels grumble about the fees but can't afford to be invisible on the site where travelers actually shop. It also earns from ads (Kayak), car rentals, flights and payments.

The scale: in 2025, travelers booked 1.235 billion room nights through its sites, on \$186 billion of total bookings, giving it \$26.9 billion in revenue.

One thing most people don't know: Booking.com isn't run from Silicon Valley. Its biggest business is based in Amsterdam.

Public (NASDAQ: BKNG). One of the most expensive stocks per share on the market. Boldly going where no hotel commission has gone before.

The Concorde flew from London to New York in about three and a half hours. It retired in 2003. Since then, every plane you've ever been on has been slower than one from the 1970s.

Boom Supersonic wants to fix that. Founder Blake Scholl, a former Amazon exec, started it in 2014 to build Overture, a new supersonic airliner that could get you from New York to London in about three and a half hours. United and American have both placed orders.

In January 2025 its test jet, XB-1, broke the sound barrier, the first independently built supersonic jet to do it. It's done it repeatedly since, in a way that kept the sonic boom from reaching the ground.

Now the plot twist nobody saw coming.

How it makes money: not from planes, yet. Overture is still years from flying passengers. So in December 2025 Boom announced a new product built from its jet engine technology: **Superpower**, a 42-megawatt natural gas turbine designed to power AI data centers. Data centers can't get enough electricity, the grid can't keep up, and the turbine waitlists are years long.

Crusoe, the AI data center company, immediately ordered 29 of them, worth about \$1.25 billion. First deliveries are planned for 2027. Boom says the turbine profits will help pay for getting Overture certified.

So the supersonic jet company is now an AI power company, and the AI boom is literally funding the return of supersonic flight.

Private. Talk about a hard pivot. Mach 1.7 of them, to be exact.

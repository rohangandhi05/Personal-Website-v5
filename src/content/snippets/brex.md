If you read the Ramp snippet, here's the other side of the rivalry.

Brex was founded in 2017 by Henrique Dubugras and Pedro Franceschi, two Brazilian founders who had already built and run a payments company in Brazil as teenagers. They moved to Silicon Valley, got into Y Combinator, and spotted a simple problem: startups couldn't get corporate credit cards, because banks wanted personal guarantees and years of history. Brex said: we'll look at how much money you raised instead.

It took off. By 2022 Brex was valued at \$12.3 billion and was the hottest fintech in tech.

How it makes money: the same core model as Ramp. Every time a company swipes a Brex card, the merchant pays an interchange fee and Brex gets a cut. Plus software for expenses, bill pay and travel.

Then came the plot twist: Ramp. Started two years later with nearly the same product, Ramp grew faster, and the two became the Coke and Pepsi of corporate cards, complete with snarky marketing and founders subtweeting each other.

In January 2026 Capital One agreed to buy Brex for \$5.15 billion in cash and stock. That's less than half its old peak valuation. Meanwhile Ramp has gone on to be valued at \$60 billion.

Still, from a teenage payments startup in Brazil to a multi-billion-dollar exit to one of America's biggest banks? Most founders would take that in a heartbeat. Franceschi is staying on to lead Brex inside Capital One.

Being acquired by Capital One (NYSE: COF). In the card wars, you win some and you get acquired by some.

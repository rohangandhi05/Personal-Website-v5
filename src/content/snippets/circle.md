Circle has one of the simplest business models in tech. Explain it to a kid in four steps:

1. You give Circle one real dollar.
2. Circle gives you one USDC, a digital dollar that lives on a blockchain and can zip around the world in seconds, any time of day.
3. Circle takes your real dollar and puts it in safe stuff like short-term U.S. Treasury bills.
4. Those Treasuries pay interest. Circle keeps the interest. You get none.

That's it. That's the business. In early 2026, about 94% of Circle's revenue came from interest on the reserves backing USDC. There was about \$75 billion of USDC in circulation at the end of 2025.

The catch most people don't know about: **Circle has to share a lot of it with Coinbase.** Coinbase helped launch USDC and gets a big cut of the interest, especially on USDC held on Coinbase. In the first half of 2026, Circle brought in about \$1.4 billion and paid \$819 million of it to distribution partners and transaction costs. The digital dollar store gives a lot of its margin to the mall.

The other catch: Circle's income depends on interest rates. When the Fed cuts rates, Circle's revenue shrinks, even if nothing about its product changes.

Its plan to fix both: Arc, its own blockchain built for payments, and growing the share of USDC that flows through Circle's own products instead of partners'.

Founded in 2013 by Jeremy Allaire and Sean Neville. Went public on the NYSE in June 2025, and the stock exploded on day one.

Public (NYSE: CRCL). A dollar for a dollar, and the interest for Circle.

In March 2000, for a brief, glorious moment, Cisco was the most valuable company in the world.

Not Microsoft. Not GE. Cisco. The company that makes the boxes that move internet traffic around. Then the dot-com bubble popped, and Cisco became the textbook example of "great company, terrible stock."

Cisco is the plumber of the internet. Its routers and switches are the pipes and valves that send data from one place to another, in offices, data centers and telecom networks. You've never seen one, but your data passes through them constantly. It was started in 1984 by Len Bosack and Sandy Lerner, a married couple who ran computer networks for different departments at Stanford and wanted their networks to talk to each other.

How it makes money: selling networking hardware, plus an increasing pile of subscriptions for software and security. In 2024 it bought Splunk, the data and security analytics company, for about \$28 billion.

Why it's interesting again: AI data centers need absurd amounts of networking to connect thousands of chips. In fiscal 2026, Cisco took \$9.3 billion in AI infrastructure orders from the giant cloud companies, about 4.5 times the year before, and expects \$7.5 billion in revenue from them in fiscal 2027.

Twenty-six years after the bubble, the plumber is getting called back to the biggest construction site in history.

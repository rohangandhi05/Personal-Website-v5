Scott Wu, Cognition's CEO, is a competitive programming legend. He won gold medals at the International Olympiad in Informatics as a teenager, where kids solve brutal algorithm puzzles against the clock. His co-founders have a similar trophy cabinet.

In March 2024 they released a demo of Devin, billed as "the first AI software engineer." Not an autocomplete. A coworker: you give it a task in Slack, and it plans, writes the code, tests it and opens a pull request. The video went viral, and every engineer on the internet had the same thought: wait, is this my replacement?

How it makes money: companies pay for Devin based on how much work it does, plus subscriptions for Windsurf, its AI code editor. Devin is sold less like a tool and more like hiring a very fast junior engineer who never sleeps.

The Windsurf story is pure drama. In July 2025 OpenAI's roughly \$3 billion deal to buy Windsurf collapsed, then Google hired away Windsurf's founders in a licensing deal, and within days Cognition swooped in and bought what was left: the product, the team and the customers. Corporate speed-dating.

The growth since: annual revenue run rate went from about \$492 million in May 2026 to nearly \$900 million by the end of the summer. In September it raised more than \$2 billion at a \$48 billion valuation. It says about 90% of its own code is now written by its AI.

In November it launched Devin Bot, a persistent agent meant to own whole projects, not just tasks.

Private. From winning coding contests to building the contestant.

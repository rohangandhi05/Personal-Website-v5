During the 2022 Super Bowl, Coinbase ran an ad that was just a QR code bouncing around a black screen, like the old DVD logo. That's it. No celebrity, no slogan.

So many people scanned it that the Coinbase app crashed. Peak crypto era.

What Coinbase is: the biggest crypto exchange in the U.S. You can buy, sell and store Bitcoin and other coins. Founded in 2012 by Brian Armstrong, a former Airbnb engineer, it went public in April 2021 as Bitcoin hit a record high. Timing.

How it makes money is where it gets interesting:

**Trading fees.** Every time you buy or sell, Coinbase takes a cut. This is the big one, and it swings with crypto prices. When crypto is hot, Coinbase prints. When it's cold, revenue drops. In the July–September 2026 quarter, with crypto prices down, revenue fell to about \$1.2 billion, from about \$1.9 billion a year earlier.

**Stablecoins.** Coinbase has a deal with Circle, the issuer of the USDC stablecoin, and earns a share of the interest on the cash backing USDC. More than a quarter of all USDC sits in Coinbase products. That's a bank-like business hiding inside a crypto app.

**Staking, custody and derivatives.** It holds crypto for big institutions, earns rewards for running parts of blockchains, and bought Deribit, a major crypto options exchange.

It also built Base, its own blockchain, which has no token, to the confusion of crypto Twitter.

Public (NASDAQ: COIN). CEO Brian Armstrong also co-founded NewLimit, a longevity startup. Buy the dip, live forever.

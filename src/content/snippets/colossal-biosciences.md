In April 2025, Colossal Biosciences introduced the world to three white, fluffy wolf pups named Romulus, Remus and Khaleesi.

The company called them dire wolves, the giant extinct wolves made famous by Game of Thrones, brought back through gene editing. Scientists argued about whether they're truly dire wolves or gray wolves with some dire wolf genes edited in. Either way, they made the cover of Time, and one of them is named after Daenerys Targaryen. Peak 2025.

Colossal is a "de-extinction" company, co-founded in 2021 by entrepreneur Ben Lamm and Harvard geneticist George Church. Its target list sounds like a museum exhibit: the woolly mammoth, the dodo and the Tasmanian tiger. Along the way it made "woolly mice," lab mice with long mammoth-style fur, as a test run. Lamm says it's on track for a mammoth calf, carried by an elephant surrogate, by late 2028.

So how does a company bringing back the mammoth make money? Mammoths aren't for sale. Yet.

**Spinouts.** The gene editing, artificial womb and computational biology tools it builds along the way are valuable on their own, so Colossal spins them out as separate companies: Form Bio for biotech software, Breaking for plastic-eating microbes, and others.

**Conservation.** The same tools can help save endangered species alive today.

Investors love the moonshot. In January 2025 it was valued at \$10.2 billion, and in 2026 it was reported to be in talks to raise at \$20 billion or more.

Private. Jurassic Park was a warning. Colossal read it as a business plan.

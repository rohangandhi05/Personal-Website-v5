At many oil wells, there's natural gas that comes up with the oil and isn't worth piping anywhere. So drillers just burn it off. Those flames you see in photos of oil fields? That's wasted energy, and a lot of pollution.

In 2018, Chase Lochmiller and Cully Cavness had an idea: put shipping containers full of computers right next to the wells, and use that gas to generate electricity on site. At first those computers mined Bitcoin.

That's Crusoe. A company that started by turning flare gas into crypto.

Then AI happened, and Crusoe realized its real skill wasn't mining Bitcoin. It was finding cheap power and building computing infrastructure fast. It sold off its Bitcoin mining business and went all in on AI.

The big one: Abilene, Texas. Crusoe built the flagship data center campus for the OpenAI and Oracle Stargate project, financed with billions from JPMorgan and Blue Owl. It's also building a second Abilene campus for Microsoft.

How it makes money, two ways:
1. **Building and leasing data centers** for giant AI customers.
2. **Its own AI cloud,** renting out GPUs to companies that need computing power.

In September 2026 it raised \$3.9 billion at a \$30.9 billion valuation, about triple its value from a year earlier. Co-founder and CEO Chase Lochmiller was on TBPN in November talking about building data centers near cheap, underused energy.

Private. Named like a castaway, built like one too: survive anywhere, use whatever you find.

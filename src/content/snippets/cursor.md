In 2022, four MIT students started a company called Anysphere. They took VS Code, the free code editor most programmers already use, copied it (legally, it's open source), and rebuilt it around AI.

They called it Cursor. It became the fastest-growing software startup anyone had ever seen.

What it does: you write code, and Cursor's AI writes alongside you. It predicts your next edit, rewrites whole files when you ask in plain English, and increasingly runs as an agent that does whole tasks while you get coffee. For many programmers, it replaced their old editor in about a week.

How it makes money: subscriptions. Individual developers pay a monthly fee, and companies pay per seat for their whole engineering team. Revenue went from about \$100 million a year in January 2025 to about \$2 billion by February 2026. That's 20x in roughly 13 months. Most companies celebrate doubling.

The twist most people don't know: Cursor doesn't just use other companies' AI models anymore. It trains its own, so it isn't totally dependent on the labs it competes with.

And the plot twist of the year: **SpaceX is buying it.** In June 2026, days after SpaceX's record IPO, it agreed to acquire Cursor in an all-stock deal worth \$60 billion, part of Elon Musk's push to make SpaceX's xAI unit competitive with OpenAI and Anthropic. Cursor had reportedly turned down OpenAI twice before.

Private, and on its way to being part of SpaceX. Four college kids built a code editor and sold it to a rocket company. Every engineering student just felt a disturbance in the Force.

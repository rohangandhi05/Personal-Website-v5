Explain Databricks like I'm five.

You have a giant toy box. Inside: every receipt, click, sensor reading and spreadsheet your company has ever made, all jumbled together. Databricks is the place where you dump the whole box, sort it, ask it questions, and train AI on it. It calls this a "lakehouse," which sounds like a vacation rental but is actually a data warehouse that also handles messy data.

Explain it like I'm fifteen.

In 2009 a group of Berkeley researchers built Apache Spark, free software for crunching enormous datasets. In 2013 they started Databricks to sell the easy, managed version. Classic move: give away the recipe, sell the restaurant.

Explain how it makes money like I'm an accountant.

Databricks runs on top of AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, and charges by usage. The more data you crunch, the more you pay. Customers don't buy seats, they burn compute, and AI workloads burn a lot of it. In August it crossed a \$7 billion revenue run rate, growing more than 80% a year, with positive free cash flow.

Explain the valuation like I'm a VC.

It closed a \$5 billion round at \$190 billion, led by Coatue, up from \$134 billion in December 2025. CEO Ali Ghodsi has spent years being asked about an IPO the way your aunt asks when you're getting married. Current answer: maybe 2027.

Private. Founded by professors, valued like a nation-state.

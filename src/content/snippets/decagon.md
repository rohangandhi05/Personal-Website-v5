"Your call is very important to us. Please hold."

Forty minutes of smooth jazz later, you're connected to someone who asks for your account number, which you already entered twice.

Decagon wants that hold music to go extinct.

It builds AI agents, which it calls "concierges," that handle customer support for companies across chat, email and voice. The AI reads the company's policies, looks up your order, processes the refund or changes the booking, and only hands off to a human when it truly needs to. Customers include Avis Budget Group, Mercado Libre and Deutsche Telekom.

It was founded in 2023 by Jesse Zhang and Ashwin Sreenivas, two founders who had each already started and sold a company.

How it makes money: companies pay based on how many conversations the AI handles or resolves. For a big business, every customer service conversation handled by AI instead of a human agent can save real money, and Decagon takes a share of that savings.

If you read the Sierra snippet, yes, these two are direct rivals, both racing to become the default AI help desk for big companies.

Decagon raised \$250 million in January 2026, led by Coatue and Index Ventures, tripling its valuation to \$4.5 billion. It doesn't disclose revenue, but one research firm estimates it passed \$100 million in annual revenue by mid-2026. Zhang came on TBPN to talk about expanding internationally, including a new office in São Paulo.

Private. Your call is important to us. Unlike before, we mean it.

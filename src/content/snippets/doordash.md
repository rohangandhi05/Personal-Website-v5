In 2013 four Stanford students put up a simple website called PaloAltoDelivery.com with menus from local restaurants and their own phone numbers. When orders came in, they drove the food over themselves.

That website became DoorDash, the biggest food delivery app in the U.S.

So how does it make money when you order a burrito? Everyone takes a bite:

**The restaurant pays a commission,** a percentage of your order, for being on the app.

**You pay a delivery fee and a service fee.** The little line items you scroll past at checkout.

**DashPass members pay a monthly subscription** to skip delivery fees. It's DoorDash's Costco card, and it keeps you from opening Uber Eats.

**Restaurants and brands pay for ads.** "Sponsored" listings at the top of the app, and promotions for grocery and retail brands. This is the sneaky growth engine: DoorDash knows exactly what millions of people order and when.

The Dasher, the person actually bringing the food, gets paid per delivery plus your tip.

It's not just restaurants anymore. DoorDash delivers groceries, pharmacy items and retail, and in 2025 it bought Deliveroo, the British delivery company, to expand overseas. Q1 2026 revenue was \$4.03 billion, up 33%.

The future: Dot, DoorDash's own small delivery robot designed for suburban streets.

Public (NASDAQ: DASH). CEO Tony Xu is one of those original four students. From delivering burritos personally to having a robot do it. The ultimate promotion.

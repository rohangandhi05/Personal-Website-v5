"EA Sports. It's in the game."

If you've ever played Madden, FIFA or NHL, that voice is burned into your skull. Electronic Arts was founded in 1982 by Trip Hawkins, who left Apple to start it, and it's been making the sports games your whole friend group argues over ever since.

Here's what changed in 2026: **EA isn't a public company anymore.**

In August, a group led by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, private equity firm Silver Lake and Affinity Partners (the investment firm run by Jared Kushner) closed a deal to buy EA for about \$55 billion. It's the largest leveraged buyout in history, with about \$20 billion of it paid for with debt. Shareholders got \$210 a share and the stock disappeared from Nasdaq.

How EA makes money, and why the buyers wanted it so badly:

**Selling games:** Madden, College Football, EA Sports FC, The Sims, Battlefield, Apex Legends.

**Live services, the real money:** Ultimate Team. You buy card packs with real money for a chance at better players. It's the trading-card business, digitized, inside a game that comes out every year. A huge share of EA's revenue comes from in-game spending like this, not the \$70 box price.

The thing most people don't know: EA lost the FIFA name in 2023 after a fight with FIFA over licensing fees. It renamed the game EA Sports FC. Players kept buying it anyway, because what they wanted was the game, not the four letters.

EA brought in about \$7.5 billion in revenue in fiscal 2026.

Private, as of August 2026. Still in the game. Just no longer on the ticker.

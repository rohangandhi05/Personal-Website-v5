In Poland, foreign movies on TV are often "dubbed" by one single, flat male voice reading every character's lines over the original audio. The hero, the villain, the love interest, the child: same guy, same monotone.

Two Polish childhood friends, Mati Staniszewski and Piotr Dąbkowski, grew up suffering through this. In 2022 they started ElevenLabs to fix it.

That's the origin of one of the biggest AI companies in Europe. Spite, against bad dubbing.

What ElevenLabs does: AI voices. It can turn text into speech that sounds genuinely human, with emotion and pauses, clone a voice from a short sample, and dub a video into dozens of languages while keeping the original speaker's voice. It's behind a huge number of audiobooks, YouTube voiceovers, games and, increasingly, AI phone agents that answer customer service calls.

How it makes money, two ways:
1. **Subscriptions** for creators, from hobbyists to studios.
2. **Enterprise and API deals** for companies building voice agents and products. This is now more than half the business.

Annual recurring revenue reached about \$350 million at the end of 2025, passed \$500 million in the first four months of 2026, and has been reported at around \$600 million since.

The valuation keeps doubling: \$11 billion in a Sequoia-led round in February 2026, then \$22 billion in a September tender offer that let employees sell shares.

Private. Every character finally has their own voice. The lone Polish narrator is out of a job.

Erewhon is named after an 1872 satirical novel by Samuel Butler. The title is "nowhere" spelled roughly backwards, about a fictional utopia.

Today it's a grocery store in Los Angeles where a smoothie costs about \$20, a tote bag is a status symbol, and people line up at 6 a.m. for store openings like it's a sneaker drop.

The history is surprisingly crunchy. Erewhon started in 1966 as a small macrobiotic natural foods shop in Boston, founded by Michio and Aveline Kushi, pioneers of the health food movement. It moved to LA, nearly disappeared, and in 2011 was bought by Tony and Josephine Antoci, who turned it into what it is now: a luxury wellness supermarket.

How it makes money: the same way any grocery store does, selling food, but at premium prices with premium margins. The real engine is the **Tonic Bar**, where celebrity collaboration smoothies became a pop culture phenomenon. Hailey Bieber's strawberry "skin" smoothie was famously selling more than 40,000 cups a month at one point. Smoothie collabs with celebrities are essentially paid ads that customers pay for.

It's also a fashion brand now. People who've never shopped there buy the merch.

It's expanding beyond its LA bubble: its 14th store opened in Thousand Oaks, more are planned, and a New York Tonic Bar was announced for November 2026. One report put average sales at around \$1 million per store per week. Its chief growth officer, Kabir Jain, has been on TBPN.

Private. Utopia, it turns out, has a \$20 smoothie.

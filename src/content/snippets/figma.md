In 2022, Adobe agreed to buy Figma for about \$20 billion.

Regulators in Europe and the UK said: absolutely not, that would kill competition. In December 2023 the deal died, and Adobe had to pay Figma a \$1 billion breakup fee.

So Figma got a billion dollars for being too good to be bought. Name a better consolation prize.

What Figma does: design software that runs in your browser. Designers use it to make apps and websites, and, crucially, everyone else on the team can jump into the same file at the same time, like Google Docs for design. You see your coworkers' cursors flying around. That multiplayer idea is why it beat older desktop tools.

How it makes money: subscriptions, mostly charged per seat. Big companies pay for every designer, developer and product manager who needs edit access. It hit about \$1.06 billion in revenue in 2025, up 41%, and has been growing even faster in 2026 as it adds AI features like its new Design Agent.

The founders: Dylan Field dropped out of Brown on a Thiel Fellowship (Peter Thiel pays young people \$100,000 to skip college) and started Figma in 2012 with Evan Wallace.

The stock story is rough, though. Figma went public in July 2025 at \$33 a share, rocketed past \$140 in days, then slid back below its IPO price as investors worried AI might make design tools less necessary.

Public (NYSE: FIG). Field has been on TBPN, and Figma sponsors the show. Still the best breakup in tech history.

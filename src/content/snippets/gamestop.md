Five years ago, GameStop was a dying mall store that Wall Street hedge funds were betting against.

Then the internet showed up.

In January 2021, Reddit's WallStreetBets, led spiritually by a YouTuber named Roaring Kitty, piled into GameStop stock. The price went from under \$20 to over \$400 in weeks. A hedge fund that had shorted it, Melvin Capital, needed a multi-billion-dollar rescue. Robinhood restricted trading. Congress held hearings. Hollywood made a movie, Dumb Money.

What happened next is the part people forgot. GameStop used the meme stock prices to sell new shares and raise cash. Lots of it. Later it issued billions in convertible bonds too. By early 2026 it had about \$9 billion in cash and investments, which is a lot for a company whose actual store business keeps shrinking.

How it makes money: still selling video games, consoles and accessories, plus an increasing amount of collectibles and trading cards. Revenue is declining as it closes stores and gamers download games instead of buying discs.

So what do you do with \$9 billion and a shrinking business? If you're CEO Ryan Cohen, the founder of Chewy:

- Buy about 4,709 Bitcoin in 2025.
- In May 2026, make an unsolicited \$55.5 billion offer to buy eBay. eBay's board called it "neither credible nor attractive."

That's like a lemonade stand offering to buy Coca-Cola, except the lemonade stand has \$9 billion in its lemonade jar.

Public (NYSE: GME). Power to the players. Diamond hands.

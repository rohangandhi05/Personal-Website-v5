Everyone thinks the bottleneck for AI is chips.

Ask anyone building a data center and they'll tell you the real bottleneck is electricity. And one of the biggest bottlenecks for electricity is a machine most people have never thought about: the gas turbine.

Enter GE Vernova.

It's the energy business that was spun out of General Electric in April 2024, when the old GE conglomerate split itself into three. It makes gas turbines, wind turbines and the grid equipment that moves power around. Around a quarter of the world's electricity is generated using its technology.

How it makes money, two ways:
1. **Selling the equipment:** turbines, generators, grid gear.
2. **Servicing it for decades:** utilities sign long service agreements to keep turbines running. These are sticky, high-margin, and quietly huge. Its services backlog alone is about \$93 billion.

Now the wild part. Demand for gas turbines has exploded because of AI data centers and electrification. GE Vernova's total backlog hit \$197 billion. It's signing turbine deals for delivery around 2030, and pricing for 2026 gas orders is almost 50% above 2025. If you want a new turbine, you're basically joining a waitlist like it's a Hermès bag.

The stock has followed. A sleepy industrial spinoff became one of the hottest AI trades on Wall Street, without making a single chip.

Public (NYSE: GEV). In a gold rush, sell shovels. In an AI rush, sell the power plant.

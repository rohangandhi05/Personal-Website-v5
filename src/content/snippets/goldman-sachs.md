In 2009, Rolling Stone famously called Goldman Sachs "a great vampire squid wrapped around the face of humanity." Goldman was not amused. Wall Street put it on a T-shirt.

Seventeen years later, the squid is having its best year ever.

What Goldman actually does, in plain English:

**Helps companies raise money.** When a company goes public, Goldman figures out the price, finds the buyers and takes a fee. It was the lead bank on the SpaceX IPO this year, one of the biggest listings in history, and is lined up as an underwriter for Anthropic too.

**Advises on mergers.** When one giant buys another, Goldman gets paid to advise, often on deals worth tens of billions.

**Trades.** Stocks, bonds, currencies and commodities, for big investors like pension funds and hedge funds.

**Manages money** for rich people and institutions.

In the April–June 2026 quarter, Goldman made a record \$20.3 billion in revenue, up 39%, with stock trading alone bringing in \$7.4 billion. The AI boom means huge IPOs, huge mergers and huge trading volumes. All of those pay Goldman.

Most people don't know: Goldman tried to become your regular bank with Marcus savings accounts and the Apple Card, lost billions on it, and has been backing out.

CEO David Solomon was also, for a while, a part-time DJ who played festivals as "D-Sol." He recently sat down with TBPN.

Public (NYSE: GS). Founded in 1869 by Marcus Goldman. Still sucking up the good deals, apparently.

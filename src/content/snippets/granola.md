You've seen it on a Zoom call: "Otter.ai Notetaker has joined the meeting." Everyone stares at the little bot square in awkward silence, and someone says "is that thing recording?"

Granola's big idea is very simple: **don't send a bot.**

Granola is an AI notepad that runs on your computer and listens to the audio from your meeting directly, the way you hear it. No bot joins, nobody gets weirded out. During the call, you jot down a few rough notes in your own words. Afterward, Granola combines your scribbles with the transcript and turns them into clean, organized notes, with what was decided and who's doing what next.

It was founded by Chris Pedregal, who had already sold his education app Socratic to Google, and Sam Stephenson. It's especially beloved by founders and investors, who are basically in meetings all day, and it quietly became one of the most talked-about AI apps in tech circles.

How it makes money: freemium subscriptions. You can try it free, and individuals and companies pay monthly for unlimited use and team features. Companies like Vanta, Gusto, Asana and Mistral now use it across their teams.

The founders admit AI meeting notes are becoming a commodity, with Zoom, Google and Microsoft all building their own. So Granola is pushing into shared team notes and AI agents that can act on everything said in your meetings.

In March 2026 it raised \$125 million at a \$1.5 billion valuation, led by Index Ventures, six times its value less than a year earlier.

Private. This meeting could have been an email. Granola made it notes instead.

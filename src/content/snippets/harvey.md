Harvey is an AI startup for lawyers, and yes, it's widely reported to be named after Harvey Specter, the slick lawyer from Suits.

Which is the most lawyer thing a startup could possibly do.

It started in 2022 when Winston Weinberg, a junior litigator at a big law firm, and his roommate Gabe Pereyra, an AI researcher who'd worked at Google DeepMind, tested an early OpenAI model on real legal questions and realized it was scarily good. One of their first backers was OpenAI's own startup fund.

What Harvey does: AI that drafts contracts, reviews documents, researches case law and summarizes thousand-page filings. The grunt work that junior associates used to spend all night doing.

How it makes money: subscriptions sold to law firms and corporate legal departments, priced per lawyer. More than 1,500 customers, including about half of America's 100 biggest law firms. Annual recurring revenue was estimated at around \$300 million by May 2026, up from about \$195 million at the end of 2025.

The delicious tension: big law firms make money by billing clients by the hour. Harvey makes lawyers dramatically faster. So every firm that buys Harvey is, in a way, buying a tool that shrinks its own billable hours. Expect a lot of creative new pricing models in legal over the next few years.

In March 2026 it raised \$200 million at an \$11 billion valuation, co-led by Sequoia and Singapore's GIC. Its main rival is Legora, from Sweden.

Private. Harvey Specter never lost a case. Harvey the startup is trying not to lose a market.

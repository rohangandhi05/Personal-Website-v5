Here's a problem most people don't know exists in space.

A big rocket like SpaceX's Falcon 9 is a great bus to low Earth orbit. But a lot of satellites need to go somewhere else: higher orbits, different angles, all the way out to geostationary orbit about 36,000 km up. The rocket drops everyone off at the same stop, and getting from there to your actual destination is a whole separate problem.

Impulse Space is the Uber from the bus stop.

It was founded in 2021 by Tom Mueller, one of SpaceX's very first employees, who designed the Merlin engines that power the Falcon 9. After 18 years building SpaceX's engines, he left to build the next piece of the puzzle himself.

What it builds:
- **Mira:** a vehicle that rides up with your satellites and then moves them around in low orbit.
- **Helios:** a powerful upper stage designed to take payloads from low orbit to geostationary orbit in under a day, a trip that can take a satellite months on its own. First launch planned for 2027.

How it makes money: charging per delivery. Satellite operators and the U.S. military pay Impulse to carry their payloads to the exact orbit they need. It has already sold out its shared Helios missions for 2028.

In 2026 it raised \$808 million total in its Series D, starting with \$500 million in June at a \$4.26 billion valuation, then a \$308 million extension in September reportedly at a higher price.

Private. The rocket gets you there. Impulse gets you there there.

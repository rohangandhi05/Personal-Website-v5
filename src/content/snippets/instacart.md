You think Instacart is a delivery app. A stranger walks the aisles of your Safeway, picks your bananas (always too green or too brown), and drops them at your door.

That's true. But it's not the most interesting part.

Instacart was founded in 2012 by Apoorva Mehta, an ex-Amazon engineer who reportedly tried around 20 startup ideas before this one stuck. It became famous during the pandemic, when it was suddenly how half of America bought groceries.

How it makes money:

**Fees.** Delivery fees, service fees and the Instacart+ membership on your side, plus fees from retailers who use it.

**Ads. This is the secret weapon.** When you search "chips" on Instacart and Doritos shows up first, Frito-Lay paid for that. Instacart sits at the exact moment people decide what to buy, which is the most valuable moment in all of advertising. In the July–September 2026 quarter, ad revenue hit \$331 million, up 23%.

**Technology for grocers.** Instacart sells grocery chains the software to run their own online stores, plus Caper smart carts: shopping carts with screens and scanners that ring you up as you shop and, yes, show you ads in the store. They're live in more than 100 cities.

Fun fact: Fidji Simo, who ran Instacart from 2021 to 2025, left to become CEO of Applications at OpenAI. Chris Rogers now runs the company. And in 2026 Instacart added an integration with Anthropic's Claude so you can build a grocery cart by chatting with an AI.

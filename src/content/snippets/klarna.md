In early 2024, Klarna made a headline that every CEO in the world read: its AI assistant was doing the work of about 700 customer service agents.

It became the poster child for "AI will replace jobs." CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski talked about it everywhere.

Then, in 2025, Klarna started hiring humans back for customer service, after Siemiatkowski admitted that focusing too much on cutting costs had hurt quality. The robots were cheaper. Customers wanted a person. The most-cited AI layoff story quietly got a sequel.

What Klarna does: buy now, pay later. You see "Pay in 4 with Klarna" at checkout, and you split a purchase into smaller payments. It was founded in 2005 in Stockholm, years before the Affirm and Afterpay era, and is one of Europe's best-known fintechs.

How it makes money:
1. **Merchants pay fees** to offer Klarna, because shoppers spend more when they can pay over time.
2. **Interest** on longer financing plans.
3. **The Klarna app**, cards and membership plans, plus advertising from brands trying to reach shoppers.

The valuation roller coaster is a story of its own. In 2021, at the peak of the startup boom, Klarna was valued at \$45.6 billion. In 2022 a down round valued it at \$6.7 billion. It finally went public on the NYSE in September 2025 at a roughly \$15 billion valuation, and the stock then fell sharply.

The business itself keeps growing: in the first quarter of 2026 revenue hit \$1 billion, up 44%, with \$33.7 billion in purchases made through Klarna.

Public (NYSE: KLAR). Smoooth payments. Bumpy stock.

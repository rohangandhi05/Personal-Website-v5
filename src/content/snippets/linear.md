Every software engineer has a Jira story. It's slow. The screens are crowded. Creating a ticket requires filling out fourteen fields, three of which nobody understands. People don't use Jira so much as survive it.

Linear is the revenge.

It's a tool for software teams to track bugs, tasks and projects, the same job as Jira. The difference is that it's ridiculously fast, beautiful and opinionated. Keyboard shortcuts for everything. No clutter. Engineers actually like using it, which in this category is like a dentist's office people look forward to visiting.

It was founded in 2019 by Karri Saarinen, Tuomas Artman and Jori Lallo, Finnish designers and engineers who had worked at Airbnb, Coinbase and Uber and were sick of bad tools. Saarinen led design at Airbnb, and it shows.

How it makes money: subscriptions per seat, with bigger plans for companies that need more security and admin controls.

The part that makes it unusual in Silicon Valley: **it barely needed venture money.** Linear has been profitable since around its second year and raised only about \$134 million total, tiny by modern startup standards. In 2026 it passed \$100 million in annual recurring revenue with more than 40,000 paying business customers. Existing customers keep expanding so fast that it says net revenue retention hit 177%.

In August it let employees sell \$99 million of shares in a tender offer at a \$2.5 billion valuation, double its previous price, without raising any new money.

Private. Moving tickets to "Done" since 2019.

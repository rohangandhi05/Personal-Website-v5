March 2023. Silicon Valley Bank, the bank of choice for startups, collapses in about 48 hours. Thousands of founders refresh their bank apps in a panic, then frantically try to move their money somewhere, anywhere, else.

A lot of that money went to Mercury.

Mercury is the bank account a huge number of startups open on day one. Founded in 2017 by Immad Akhund, it gives companies a clean, fast, software-first business bank account, and it's beautiful in a way that makes old bank websites feel like tax forms. About one in three U.S. startups bank with it, and it has more than 300,000 customers overall.

The twist most people don't know: **Mercury hasn't actually been a bank.** It's a fintech that partners with real banks, Choice Financial and Column, which legally hold your deposits. Mercury designs the experience on top. In April 2026 it got conditional approval from the OCC, the federal bank regulator, to become a real national bank, Mercury Bank. It still needs other approvals before it can open.

How it makes money:
1. **Card fees.** Interchange on debit and credit card spending.
2. **Deposits.** A share of the interest earned on the billions customers keep in their accounts.
3. **Treasury and software.** Investing idle cash, bill pay, invoicing.

It reported about \$650 million in annualized revenue in late 2025, and in May 2026 raised \$200 million at a \$5.2 billion valuation, led by TCV.

Private. Named after the messenger god. Currently waiting on a letter from the regulators.

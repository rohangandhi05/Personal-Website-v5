America's biggest memory chipmaker started in the basement of a dentist's office in Boise, Idaho, in 1978. Some of its early funding came from J.R. Simplot, the potato billionaire who supplied McDonald's fries.

So yes: the AI boom runs partly on french fry money.

What Micron makes: memory. DRAM, the short-term memory every computer needs, and NAND, the storage in your phone and SSD. And now the hottest product in tech, HBM (high-bandwidth memory), stacked memory that sits right next to AI chips and feeds them data fast enough to keep up.

How it makes money: selling those chips. The memory business is famously brutal, booming and busting with supply. Right now it's booming like never before.

In the quarter that ended in early September, Micron made \$54.2 billion in revenue, almost five times the year before, with \$37.7 billion in profit. Gross margin: 86.8%. For a company that manufactures physical chips, that's software-company money. Management says most of next year's HBM is already sold out and the memory shortage will get worse before it gets better.

The thing most PC gamers noticed: Micron shut down Crucial, its consumer RAM and SSD brand. Why sell memory to gamers when AI companies are paying anything you ask?

The shortage is spilling into everything: higher prices on phones, laptops, even Raspberry Pis.

Public (NASDAQ: MU). Started in a dentist's basement, now pulling teeth from every AI lab on the planet.

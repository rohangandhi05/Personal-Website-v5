Your grandma's favorite phone game is made by the company that makes Excel.

Microsoft bought Activision Blizzard for \$69 billion in 2023, and Activision owned King, the maker of Candy Crush. So yes: Call of Duty, World of Warcraft, Candy Crush and Minecraft are all Microsoft. So are LinkedIn and GitHub.

A quick game of "wait, Microsoft owns that?":

- LinkedIn, where people post "I'm humbled to announce"
- GitHub, where most of the world's code lives
- Minecraft, the best-selling video game of all time
- Xbox, which, fine, you knew

But here's the twist: none of those are the money machine. The money machine is boring, and that's the point.

Microsoft made \$331.8 billion in revenue in fiscal 2026, up 18%. The engine is Microsoft Cloud: Azure (renting out computing power and AI to businesses) plus the Office and Teams subscriptions your employer pays for every month whether you open PowerPoint or not. Azure alone passed \$100 billion in a year for the first time.

The other quiet engine: OpenAI. Microsoft was its earliest big backer, and that stake added about \$5 billion in net gains to Microsoft's profit last fiscal year.

Meanwhile gaming, the part everyone talks about, has been the weak spot, with Xbox content and services revenue shrinking.

In 2000, Netflix offered to sell itself to Blockbuster for \$50 million.

Blockbuster passed. Reportedly, its executives laughed. Blockbuster is now one store in Bend, Oregon, and a tourist attraction.

Quick refresher on what Netflix was: DVDs by mail. You picked movies on a website, red envelopes showed up in your mailbox, you mailed them back. Then in 2007 it started streaming, and then it started making its own shows, and then everyone in Hollywood started copying it.

How it makes money: overwhelmingly subscriptions. Monthly fees from hundreds of millions of members. The newer piece is advertising. The cheaper plan with ads launched in 2022, brought in over \$1.5 billion in ad revenue in 2025, and Netflix is aiming for about \$3 billion in 2026. It's also gone after live events: NFL games on Christmas, boxing, and baseball in Japan.

The funniest story of 2026: Netflix agreed to buy Warner Bros., home of Harry Potter, Batman and HBO. Then Paramount Skydance swooped in with a bigger offer and won. Netflix walked away with a \$2.8 billion breakup fee.

So Netflix tried to buy one of the most famous studios in Hollywood, lost, and got paid \$2.8 billion for its trouble. That's like losing at Monopoly and the winner Venmos you for your time.

Full-year 2026 revenue is expected around \$51 billion.

Public (NASDAQ: NFLX). Are you still watching? Netflix is. Always.

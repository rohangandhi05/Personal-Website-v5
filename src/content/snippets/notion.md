In 2015, Notion was almost dead.

The product wasn't working, the money was running out, and co-founders Ivan Zhao and Simon Last had to lay off their small team. So they did something no pitch deck would recommend: they moved to Kyoto, Japan, where life was cheaper, and spent months rebuilding the entire app from scratch.

When they came back, they had the Notion people know now: a blank page that can turn into notes, docs, wikis, to-do lists, databases and project trackers. It's the Lego set of productivity apps. Students plan their semesters in it. Startups run their entire companies in it. Somebody on TikTok is selling a \$40 "Notion life planner template" right now.

How it makes money: freemium. Individuals use it free. Teams and companies pay per member per month for collaboration, admin controls and more storage. The AI features push people onto pricier plans.

The 2026 move is AI agents. Custom Agents launched in February and users built more than a million of them in two months: little AI workers that update your docs, answer questions about your company wiki and handle busywork. In May it started charging for agent usage, its first pay-as-you-go revenue.

Notion says it has about 100 million users. Revenue estimates are high and rising: one research firm put it on track for about \$865 million in 2026. In January a share sale for employees valued the company at \$11 billion.

Private. Proof that sometimes the best pivot is a plane ticket.

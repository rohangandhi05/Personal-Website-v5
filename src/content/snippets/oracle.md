**Q: What is Oracle?**
A database company. Founded in 1977 by Larry Ellison and two co-founders. Its very first big customer was the CIA, and the company is named after a CIA project codenamed "Oracle."

**Q: How does it make money?**
For decades, by selling database software to big companies and then charging them maintenance and support fees every year, more or less forever. Switching databases is like moving houses with all your furniture on fire, so customers rarely leave. Oracle also owns a pile of business software, like NetSuite and the hospital records company Cerner.

**Q: So why is it in the AI conversation?**
Because Oracle went all in on renting out AI data centers. It's a key builder of OpenAI's Stargate project, with a flagship site in Abilene, Texas.

**Q: How all in?**
Its pile of signed future contracts was reported at \$664 billion as of its latest quarter. Earlier this year, about \$300 billion of a \$553 billion backlog was tied to OpenAI alone.

**Q: Is that a lot of eggs in one basket?**
It's most of the eggs, and the basket is borrowing money. Oracle raised \$43 billion in debt in its last fiscal year and is spending more on data centers than it brings in, betting the contracts pay off.

**Q: Anything else fun?**
Larry Ellison owns roughly 98% of the Hawaiian island of Lanai. He bought an island. Then he bought a data center bet bigger than most islands' economies.

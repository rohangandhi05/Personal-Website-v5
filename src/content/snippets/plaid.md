You've used Plaid if you've ever:

- Linked your bank account to Venmo
- Connected your bank to Robinhood or Coinbase
- Hooked your checking account up to a budgeting app
- Seen a little pop-up that says "Select your bank" and typed in your login

That pop-up is Plaid. It's the invisible plumbing that lets financial apps talk to your bank. Most people have used it dozens of times without ever learning its name.

Founded in 2013 by Zach Perret and William Hockey, it solved a painful problem: every bank had its own messy system, so fintech apps couldn't easily connect to them. Plaid built one connection that works with thousands of banks.

How it makes money: apps pay Plaid per connection, per verification or per month, depending on the product. Fraud checks, identity verification, account balances, payments. Revenue was estimated at around \$546 million a year in 2025.

The drama most people missed:

**The Visa deal that died.** In 2020 Visa agreed to buy Plaid for \$5.3 billion. The Justice Department sued to block it, arguing Visa was buying a future rival to kill it. The deal was called off.

**The banks fought back.** Big banks don't love apps pulling their customers' data for free. In 2025 JPMorgan started charging data aggregators for access, and Plaid agreed to pay.

Its valuation went from \$13.4 billion in 2021 down to about \$6.1 billion in 2025, then back up to \$8 billion in early 2026.

Private. You've typed your bank password into its little window. It knows you better than you think.

Ramp's entire pitch: help companies spend less money.

Ramp's entire business model: companies spending money.

This is not a contradiction. This is a business.

Ramp gives companies corporate cards plus software for expenses, bill pay, travel and procurement. Every time an employee swipes a Ramp card, the merchant pays a small fee called interchange, and Ramp gets a cut. Add fees on bill payments and a paid software tier, and you get roughly \$2 billion in annualized revenue as of October 2026.

The clever part is that the software is basically free, and it's genuinely good at finding waste: the duplicate Figma seats, the SaaS subscription a guy who left in 2023 is still paying for. Ramp tells you to cancel it. You trust Ramp more. You move more spending onto Ramp cards. Ramp earns more. It's the Costco of finance: happy to lose money on the hot dog.

Founders Eric Glyman and Karim Atiyeh had already done this once. Their first startup, Paribus, got you money back when prices dropped after you bought something. Capital One bought it.

In October Ramp raised \$1.85 billion at a \$60 billion valuation, led by Dragoneer, four months after raising at \$44 billion. That's a company adding \$16 billion in value in less time than a summer internship.

Private. Backed by Founders Fund, Thrive, Dragoneer and roughly every VC who has ever expensed a lunch. You've also heard them on TBPN, because Ramp sponsors it. Which, to be fair, is the company spending money. On brand.

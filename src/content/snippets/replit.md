A startup montage, Replit edition.

**2016.** Amjad Masad, who grew up in Jordan and later worked at Codecademy and Facebook, starts Replit with Haya Odeh. The idea: code in your browser, no setup. Students and hobbyists love it. Revenue is small.

**2017–2023.** The grind. Replit becomes a beloved tool for learning to code. Teachers use it. Kids make games on it. It raises money, but the business never really explodes. Startup Twitter respects it, the way you respect the guy at the gym who's been doing the same workout for seven years.

**Late 2024.** Replit launches Agent. You describe an app in plain English ("a booking site for my dog-walking business"), and the AI builds it, sets up the database and puts it online. No coding required. "Vibe coding," people start calling it.

**Then the montage music changes.** Annual revenue goes from about \$2.8 million to about \$150 million in less than a year. By April 2026 it's estimated at over \$500 million, and Replit is aiming for \$1 billion by the end of the year.

How it makes money: subscriptions, plus usage. The more work the AI agent does for you, the more you pay. It turns out regular people and companies will happily pay to build software without learning to code.

In March 2026 Replit raised \$400 million at a \$9 billion valuation, tripling its value in six months and making Masad a billionaire.

Private. Overnight success, nine years in the making. Cue the training montage music.

Robinhood made stock trading free in 2013. So how does a company that charges you nothing make over a billion dollars a quarter?

Pull up a chair.

**The original trick: payment for order flow.** When you place a trade, Robinhood sends it to a big trading firm like Citadel Securities, which executes it and pays Robinhood for the privilege. The firm profits on the tiny gap between buy and sell prices. You pay no commission. Everyone wins, the argument goes. Critics disagree, loudly.

**The infamous week.** January 2021, the GameStop frenzy. Robinhood restricted buying of the meme stocks, users were furious, and founder Vlad Tenev ended up testifying to Congress. The name, inspired by the legend of stealing from the rich to give to the poor, did not age well that week.

**The comeback.** Robinhood turned into a full financial super-app: Gold subscriptions, cash interest, retirement accounts with matching, credit cards, crypto trading.

**The plot twist: betting.** Robinhood now offers prediction markets, letting users trade contracts on real events like elections, sports and economic data. In the July–September 2026 quarter, event contracts brought in \$173 million, more than crypto's \$120 million. It even built its own prediction-market exchange, Rothera, with trading firm Susquehanna.

Total revenue that quarter: \$1.42 billion.

Founded by Stanford friends Vlad Tenev and Baiju Bhatt, a math guy and a physics guy. Fitting, because the business model has a lot of moving parts.

Public (NASDAQ: HOOD). Free to use. Not free to run.

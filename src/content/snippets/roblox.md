Roblox is not a game. That's the first thing to understand.

It's a platform where millions of people build their own games, and everyone else plays them. Adopt Me, Grow a Garden, Brookhaven: none of those were made by Roblox. They were made by players, often teenagers, some of whom became millionaires.

How the money works:

1. Players buy Robux, the in-game currency, with real money. Mostly kids, with their parents' cards. This is the source of basically all revenue.
2. Players spend Robux inside games made by creators: skins, pets, power-ups, a slightly faster sword.
3. Roblox keeps a cut, and creators can cash out their Robux for real dollars through the "Developer Exchange."

In 2025, creators earned over \$1.5 billion through that exchange. Somewhere a 16-year-old is making more from a pet-collecting game than their parents make from their jobs, and the family dinner conversations must be fascinating.

Founded in 2004 by David Baszucki, who still runs it.

The hard part right now is safety. After lawsuits and criticism over how well the platform protected kids, Roblox rolled out mandatory age checks. By the end of the July–September 2026 quarter, 64% of daily users worldwide had verified their age. It's working on trust, and it's costing growth: in that quarter revenue was up 16% to \$1.57 billion, but bookings fell 12% and daily users dropped to 138 million from a year earlier. Roblox is betting it can grow back with older players, and now pays creators 42% more when verified adults spend in their games.

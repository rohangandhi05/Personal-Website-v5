Salesforce's original mascot was the word "software" with a red line through it, like the Ghostbusters logo.

The joke: in 1999, business software came on CDs, took months to install and needed a server room. Marc Benioff, an ex-Oracle exec, said you should just log in through a browser and pay a monthly fee. The industry laughed. Then the industry became Salesforce.

What it does: CRM, short for customer relationship management. Translation: it's the giant digital Rolodex where companies track every customer, every sales call and every deal. If you've ever gotten a suspiciously well-timed follow-up email from a salesperson, Salesforce probably reminded them.

How it makes money: subscriptions, mostly charged per user per month. A sales team of 500 pays for 500 seats. It also owns Slack, Tableau and MuleSoft, and recently bought Informatica for data management.

The 2026 pitch is Agentforce: AI agents that handle customer service and sales tasks on their own. Agentforce passed \$1 billion in annual recurring revenue in its first fiscal quarter of 2027. Which creates a fun tension: if AI agents replace human workers, Salesforce has to stop charging per human.

Fiscal 2026 revenue: \$41.5 billion.

Other things you might not know: Salesforce Tower is the tallest building in San Francisco, and its yearly Dreamforce conference takes over the city so completely that hotel prices go to the moon.

Public (NYSE: CRM). No software. Just \$41.5 billion of it.

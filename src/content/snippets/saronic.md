The U.S. Navy has a ship problem. China builds far more ships, far faster, and a new American warship can take the better part of a decade to go from drawing to water.

Saronic's pitch: what if a lot of the fleet didn't need anyone on board at all?

Saronic builds autonomous surface vessels: boats, from small fast attack craft to much bigger ships, that navigate, avoid collisions and carry out missions by themselves, coordinated by software. No crew means smaller, cheaper boats that can be built in huge numbers and sent where you'd never risk sailors.

It was founded in Austin in 2022, and its co-founders include Dino Mavrookas, a former Navy SEAL.

How it makes money: selling its vessels and software to the U.S. Navy and allied militaries. In December 2025 it won a \$392 million Navy contract to produce uncrewed vessels, and in September 2026 it was one of three companies picked to build the Navy's new landing craft.

The big swing is **Port Alpha**: a more than \$3 billion shipyard Saronic broke ground on in Brownsville, Texas, in September 2026, designed to eventually build vessels up to 850 feet long. That's not a startup buying some boats. That's a startup building a shipyard, the kind of thing America has mostly stopped doing.

In March 2026 it raised \$1.75 billion at a \$9.25 billion valuation, led by Kleiner Perkins.

Private. Remember the line from Jaws, "You're gonna need a bigger boat"? Saronic heard that and built a shipyard.

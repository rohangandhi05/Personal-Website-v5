Remember the Nvidia snippet, where three engineers started the company at a Denny's in 1993?

One of the first investors to back them was Sequoia Capital.

Sequoia was founded in 1972 by Don Valentine, who'd worked in the early chip industry. Its track record is the closest thing venture capital has to a dynasty: early bets on Apple, Atari, Cisco, Oracle, Nvidia, Google, YouTube, PayPal, Instagram, WhatsApp, Airbnb, Stripe and DoorDash. It's hard to pick up your phone without touching something Sequoia funded.

How it makes money: like other venture firms, a yearly fee on the money it manages plus a share of the profits when its companies win. Its edge has always been reputation: founders often want Sequoia specifically.

Not every bet worked. In 2022 Sequoia wrote its entire investment in the crypto exchange FTX down to zero after it collapsed, and apologized to its own investors.

In 2021 it made a big structural change: instead of raising a new fund every few years, it combined things into a single permanent "evergreen" fund, so it could hold great companies for decades instead of selling after ten years. It's been debated ever since.

Leadership changed recently too: in November 2025, longtime leader Roelof Botha stepped aside, and partners Alfred Lin and Pat Grady became co-leaders. Sequoia is still investing heavily in AI and backs a lot of the companies in this series, including Harvey, ElevenLabs and Notion.

Private. The tree in its name can live for thousands of years. So far, so good.

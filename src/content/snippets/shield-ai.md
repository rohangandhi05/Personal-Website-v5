In 2020, the U.S. military ran a contest called the AlphaDogfight Trials: AI programs fighting each other in simulated F-16 dogfights, with the winner taking on a real, experienced Air Force pilot in a simulator.

The winning AI beat the human pilot 5–0. Maverick would be furious.

That AI was made by a small company called Heron Systems. The next year, Shield AI bought it.

Shield AI was founded in 2015 by brothers Ryan and Brandon Tseng (Brandon is a former Navy SEAL) and Andrew Reiter. Its core product is **Hivemind**, AI software that can fly aircraft on its own, even when GPS and communications are jammed. That last part matters a lot: in modern war zones like Ukraine, jamming is everywhere, and drones that depend on a remote pilot or GPS just fall out of the sky.

How it makes money:
1. **Selling drones.** The V-BAT, a drone that takes off and lands vertically on its tail, sells for roughly \$1 million each. It's flown more than 130 missions in Ukraine.
2. **Operating drones** as a contractor for customers.
3. **Licensing Hivemind** to put inside other companies' aircraft.

The next big thing is the X-BAT, unveiled in 2025: a stealth fighter drone that can launch vertically without a runway and is flown entirely by Hivemind. It's targeting production in 2029.

In March 2026 it raised \$1.5 billion plus another \$500 million in preferred equity at a \$12.7 billion valuation, and it expects revenue to grow more than 80% this year.

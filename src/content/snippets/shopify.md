In 2004 a German programmer in Ottawa named Tobi Lütke wanted to sell snowboards online. Every e-commerce tool he tried was terrible, so he built his own.

The snowboard shop was called Snowdevil. It did fine. The software he built to run it became Shopify, one of the biggest tech companies Canada has ever produced. Very Canadian origin story: started because of winter.

What it does: Shopify lets anyone open an online store without hiring engineers. Pick a theme, add products, take payments. It powers everyone from a candle maker in her garage to huge brands, plus checkout, shipping, point-of-sale terminals for physical shops, and marketing tools.

How it makes money, and here's what most people get wrong: the monthly subscription fee is the small part. About 78% of revenue comes from "merchant solutions," mostly payments. When you buy something from a Shopify store, Shopify takes a slice of the transaction. So Shopify grows when its merchants grow. Their success is literally its revenue.

In the July–September 2026 quarter: \$3.7 billion in revenue, up 32%, with \$119 billion of goods sold through its stores. Shop Pay, its one-tap checkout, grew around 50%.

The newest twist: AI shopping. Orders coming from AI search tripled year over year, and shoppers have started letting personal AI agents buy things for them through Shop Pay. Small numbers for now, but Shopify plans to be the checkout counter for robots too.

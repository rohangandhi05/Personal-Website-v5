Most robot companies build a robot. Skild AI is building the brain, and wants it to work in any robot.

A robot arm in a warehouse, a four-legged robot dog, a humanoid walking around a factory: today each one usually needs its own custom software, built from scratch. Skild's idea is one general AI model, which it calls an "omni-bodied" brain, that can be dropped into all of them and figure out how to move, grab and walk.

Think of it like a universal remote, except instead of controlling your TV it controls the robot, and instead of buttons it has years of training data.

It was founded in 2023 in Pittsburgh by Carnegie Mellon professors Deepak Pathak and Abhinav Gupta, two of the most respected robotics AI researchers around.

How it makes money: licensing its robot brain to companies that build or use robots, mostly in warehouses and manufacturing. It reportedly passed \$100 million in annual recurring revenue by September 2026, about ten months after its first commercial deployment, with more than 60 paying customers. In April 2026 it bought the robotics division of Zebra Technologies, formerly Fetch Robotics, to get more robots working in real warehouses.

Investors went big. In January 2026 Skild raised about \$1.4 billion at a valuation above \$14 billion, led by SoftBank, with Nvidia and Jeff Bezos's investment firm joining in. That's roughly triple its valuation from eight months earlier.

Private. Every robot uprising movie starts with a single brain controlling all the bodies. This one is going to warehouses.

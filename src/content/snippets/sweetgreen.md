Sweetgreen sells \$15 salads to people with lanyards.

That's the bit. But there's a more interesting company underneath it.

It was started in 2007 by three Georgetown students, Jonathan Neman, Nicolas Jammet and Nathaniel Ru, who couldn't find a healthy place to eat near campus. They opened a tiny salad shop in Georgetown. Now there are hundreds of locations, and the "Sweetgreen line" at lunch is a recognized unit of time in New York and DC.

How it makes money: selling salads and bowls, in store and through the app. That's it. A restaurant business at heart, which means thin margins and a lot of lettuce.

The part most people don't know: **some Sweetgreens are run by robots.** In 2021 it bought Spyce, a robotic kitchen startup founded by MIT grads, and developed the "Infinite Kitchen," an automated assembly line that drops greens, grains and toppings into your bowl. About 32 locations had one by early 2026. In late 2025 Sweetgreen sold Spyce itself to Wonder for about \$186 million, but kept using the robot kitchens through a licensing deal.

The hard part: it's been a rough stretch. Same-store sales fell 7.9% in 2025 and kept falling into 2026, as people pulled back on pricey lunches. Q2 2026 revenue was \$192.7 million.

Co-founder and CEO Jonathan Neman went on TBPN in November to talk about the new dinner menu, AI ordering and drone delivery.

Public (NYSE: SG). Lettuce pray for a turnaround.

Most conferences happen in a hotel ballroom with lukewarm coffee. TomorrowX rented the Moody Center.

That's the basketball arena in Austin, Texas, filled with 5,000 people, per Ron Biscardi, CEO of iConnections, who put it on. His pitch to TBPN: "This is the biggest AI conference in the country right now."

The origin story is peak tech. Biscardi partnered with Antonio Gracias and Gavin Baker, who called him in January wanting to do it by October or November. His reply: "Guys, this is an 18-month kind of game." They did it anyway.

The format is borrowed from Wall Street. Biscardi compared it to Goldman's and Morgan Stanley's tech conferences, where CEOs are the stars and investors fly in to hear them.

The twist: cheap tickets and "a ton of comps" for X posters and Substackers. In his words, "an institutional event in the banking genre delivered and accessible to the everyday investor." Wall Street's velvet rope, now with blue checkmarks.

The lineup backed it up: David Solomon, Brian Armstrong, Travis Kalanick, Jon Gray, Scott Wu, Andrew Feldman. The hosts' only complaint: no CO2 cannons.

It also landed in the week of what TBPN called "a major ouster of a CEO, a giant IPO." Biscardi, deadpan: "People don't realize we were the mastermind behind the whole thing."

Fyre Festival promised supermodels and a beach. TomorrowX promised CEOs and an arena, then actually delivered.

"You don't need a Michelin chef to make you breakfast. It's okay to have some eggs on toast."

That's Andrew Feldman, CEO of Cerebras, on TBPN at TomorrowX, settling one of AI's biggest arguments with a brunch menu.

The argument: does the future run on one giant model that does everything, or a crew of small specialists? Feldman calls it a fight "for the hearts and minds of the AI community."

The hosts pitched the one-model dream as "the 10-in-one shampoo that you can brush your teeth with." Feldman: "That's exactly right."

His bet is both, with a router in the middle. Easy requests go to a small, fast model. Hard ones go to the frontier model. You don't fire up GPT-level genius to ask what time it is.

The hosts' version of the end state: some on-device inference, some wafer-scale, some big GPU racks, all working together. "Use every bit of silicon, use every power source combined." Feldman: "100% agree."

The future of AI looks less like a tasting menu and more like a diner. Something on the menu for everyone, and it comes out quick.

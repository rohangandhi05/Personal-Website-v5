750 tokens a second.

That's how fast Cerebras runs OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol in its [Ultrafast tier](https://www.unite.ai/cerebras-runs-openais-gpt-5-6-sol-at-750-tokens-per-second-in-new-ultrafast-tier/), launched in August. The answer shows up faster than you can read it. A lot faster.

Andrew Feldman, co-founder and CEO of Cerebras, brought the chip to TBPN at TomorrowX and did not undersell it: "This is the largest chip in the history of the computer industry. The fastest AI chip."

The trick is size. Everyone else cuts a silicon wafer into dozens of small chips. Cerebras keeps it whole, one chip the size of a dinner plate, and builds the server and liquid cooling around it. Feldman: "You need to bring a powered shell and we'll take care of everything."

Why speed matters: "We specialize in fast inference," he said. Inference is the AI answering, not learning. In cybersecurity, "a lot of damage can be done in minutes or even seconds," so whoever spots the attack first wins.

And they're not easing off the gas. A new hardware release about every year, "doubling performance in each one."

Nvidia sells the most chips. Cerebras sells the fastest one. As Ricky Bobby taught us, if you ain't first, you're last.

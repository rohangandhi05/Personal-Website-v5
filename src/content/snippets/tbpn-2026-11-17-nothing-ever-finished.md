Two business dinners a night. Meetings at 9 a.m. on Saturdays.

That's the AI industry right now, according to Andrew Feldman, CEO of Cerebras. "The intensity in this industry right now is unbelievable," he said on TBPN at TomorrowX. "I've been an entrepreneur for the past 27 years. We've never seen anything like it."

The hosts asked about cadence. Jensen ran Nvidia on keynotes and an annual release cycle. Brian Chesky makes Airbnb ship for a keynote. Does Cerebras need the same?

Feldman's answer came from his dad: "My dad's a professor and he used to say if there weren't conferences, nothing ever would be finished."

Every student who has ever opened the essay doc at 11:58 p.m. just nodded.

His point: public events "force clear thinking," force releases, and "create hard deadlines." Cerebras plans a new hardware release about every year, "doubling performance in each one."

Parkinson's Law says work expands to fill the time available. The fix, apparently, is to book an arena.

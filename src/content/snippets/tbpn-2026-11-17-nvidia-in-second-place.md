Everyone knows Nvidia. Far fewer people know the company beating it in a race.

That company is Cerebras. Its CEO, Andrew Feldman, carried the chip onto TBPN at TomorrowX like a proud dad with a newborn: "This is the largest chip in the history of the computer industry. The fastest AI chip."

What it does: it runs AI. Every time you ask ChatGPT something, a chip somewhere has to work out the answer one word at a time. Cerebras's whole business is doing that faster than anyone else.

How fast? Cerebras runs OpenAI's top model, GPT-5.6 Sol, at [up to 750 tokens a second](https://www.unite.ai/cerebras-runs-openais-gpt-5-6-sol-at-750-tokens-per-second-in-new-ultrafast-tier/). A token is roughly a word, so that's about a full page of text every second. You read about four words a second. You are the bottleneck now.

How it beats Nvidia, part one: size. A normal Nvidia chip fits in your palm. Cerebras uses an entire dinner-plate-sized slab of silicon as one chip, with 4 trillion transistors on it. It's the Shaquille O'Neal of chips.

Part two, the real trick: memory. On Nvidia's chips, the AI's "brain" sits in separate memory, so the chip keeps running back and forth to fetch it. Cerebras puts 44 GB of memory right on the chip itself.

Think of a chef who has to run to the grocery store for every ingredient, versus one with everything already on the counter. Gordon Ramsay knows which kitchen he'd rather yell in.

The scoreboard: on Nov 3, independent tester Artificial Analysis clocked Cerebras at [1,671 tokens a second](https://www.cerebras.ai/glossary/what-is-the-fastest-ai-inference-platform) on a popular open AI model. The next-fastest provider managed 704. That's not a photo finish.

And its new machine, the CS-4, packs three of these giant chips into one system. Cerebras says it's [up to 30 times faster](https://www.cerebras.ai/press-release/cerebras-unveils-cs-4-up-to-30-times-faster-than-gpu-based-solutions-3347356) than setups built on regular GPUs. That's the company's own number, so a pinch of salt.

Why speed matters: nobody likes waiting. Nobody is nostalgic for dial-up. And sometimes seconds are everything. In cybersecurity, Feldman said, "a lot of damage can be done in minutes or even seconds." The AI that spots the hacker first wins.

Speed also changes what AI can do. When answers come back instantly, AI can draft, double-check and fix its own work while you wait, instead of making you come back tomorrow.

So, is Nvidia in second place? In sales, not even close. In speed, Cerebras is holding the trophy. As Ricky Bobby said, if you ain't first, you're last. Though with Nvidia's money, last place looks pretty comfortable.

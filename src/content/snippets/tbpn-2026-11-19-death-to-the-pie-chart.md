"Pie charts don't create any value whatsoever."

That's Strauss Zelnick, CEO of Take-Two, on TBPN, on the day his company launched GTA VI. The question: what is AI actually doing inside a company like his?

Not mass layoffs. "For those who think you're going to vastly reduce your headcount overnight, that clearly is not happening," he said.

Instead, people are using it "to make their work less mundane, more interesting, more value added." It does come with a new line item: "we are spending money on tokens."

His bigger take: "New technology doesn't tend to reduce employment. It tends to increase productivity, which increases GDP, which actually increases employment."

So where is AI saving time? In "areas of work that no one's ever going to miss." Exhibit A: "it's quicker to make a pie chart than it used to be."

Somewhere, a McKinsey analyst felt a chill.

"They're nice to look at, but they just don't create value. So if we could have our people spend a lot less time on pie charts, I'd be thrilled."

The man just shipped the most anticipated game ever made, and his AI wish list is fewer slides. Relatable.

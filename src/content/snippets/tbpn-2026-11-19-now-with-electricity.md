In the late 1800s, companies bragged in the financial papers that they used electricity.

Some with nothing to do with electricity renamed themselves "XYZ Electricity Company." A century later, everyone slapped ".com" on their name. Burgers.com, as the man put it.

That's how Strauss Zelnick, CEO of Take-Two, answered the AI question on TBPN, more clearly than most AI CEOs manage.

"AI is going to be everywhere, and you know, we don't walk into an office today and look around and say, wow, it's amazing, you have electric lights." His word for where AI ends up: "backdrop."

The part people forget, he said: "everyone's gonna have access to the same technology." A rising tide lifts all ships.

And on the fear that AI kills big entertainment companies: "Competitive dynamics might shift, entry barriers might be reduced, but making hits, marketing hits, financing hits, employing creative talent, all that's going to remain very, very difficult."

Translation: anyone can buy a guitar. There's still only one Taylor Swift.

Give it ten years and "AI-powered" on a pitch deck will sound like "now with electricity!" on a toaster.

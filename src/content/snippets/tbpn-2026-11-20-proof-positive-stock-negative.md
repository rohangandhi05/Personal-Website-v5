Negative \$121 billion.

That's what the Riemann hypothesis is worth, according to a poster TBPN read on air. It's the pre-market drop in Anthropic's value the morning a possible proof, found with Claude's help, went viral.

The hosts were clear it's a joke: "People are not trading public market stocks based on math progress." Scientific American called the proof unverified and still needing expert review.

But the day was ugly. Per the show, Anthropic was down 10%, at \$87, barely above its \$83 IPO price. The Nasdaq and S&P were way down too.

The hosts framed the real split. Silicon Valley says AI is different this time, and self-improving models change everything. Wall Street's read: "You're a SaaS business with high capex. Not sure I like it that much."

Then the stat that stings: year to date, the hosts noted, the Nasdaq has underperformed T-bills. Boring government bonds beat tech this year. Nobody had that on their bingo card in January.

Their diagnosis wasn't a popped bubble. It was everything else: "Nvidia chaos, geopolitical chaos, cyber attacks chaos." The math isn't priced in, "just yet, but maybe soon."

Mathematicians spent 167 years on the Riemann hypothesis. Wall Street gave it one trading session.

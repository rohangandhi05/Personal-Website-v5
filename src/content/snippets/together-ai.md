Some of the best AI models in the world are free.

Meta's Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen, Mistral: anyone can download them. But "free" comes with a catch. These models are enormous, and running them takes racks of expensive GPUs that most companies don't own and don't want to manage.

Together AI's business is that catch.

It runs a cloud built specifically for open-source AI. Developers and companies send their requests to Together's API, it runs the model on its GPUs, and it charges per token, basically per word the AI reads and writes. Companies that want more can rent dedicated GPU clusters to train or fine-tune their own models.

So the models are free. The electricity, chips and engineering to run them fast and cheap are not, and that's what Together sells. It's the landlord renting out apartments in buildings it didn't design.

It was founded in 2022 by Vipul Ved Prakash (who previously founded the anti-spam company Cloudmark and sold the social search startup Topsy to Apple), along with Ce Zhang, Stanford professor Percy Liang and Chris Ré.

The growth has been huge as more companies decide they'd rather not depend entirely on OpenAI or Anthropic. Together says its annual bookings passed \$1.15 billion. In July 2026 it raised \$800 million at an \$8.3 billion valuation, led by Aramco Ventures, the venture arm of the Saudi oil giant.

Private. The world's biggest oil company is betting on the world's open-source AI. The energy business really is everywhere.

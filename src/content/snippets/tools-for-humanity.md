There's a shiny chrome sphere, about the size of a bowling ball, sitting in storefronts in Austin, Atlanta, Los Angeles, Miami, Nashville and San Francisco. You walk up, look into it, it scans your eyes, and it confirms one thing: you're a human, not a bot.

That's the Orb. It's made by Tools for Humanity, the company behind a project called World, formerly Worldcoin. It was co-founded in 2019 by CEO Alex Blania, Max Novendstern and Sam Altman, yes, the OpenAI Sam Altman.

The pitch: as AI gets better at writing, talking and making videos, it'll become nearly impossible to tell what's real online. Bots will flood dating apps, social media and elections. So World wants to give every human a digital "proof of personhood," called World ID, by scanning their iris once. Then apps can check that you're a real, unique person without knowing who you are. The company says the eye scan is encrypted and the images aren't stored on the Orb.

It's already being plugged into apps: World announced partnerships including a Tinder pilot to verify real people and a Visa card.

How does it make money? Honestly, it's still a work in progress. Signing up earns you WLD, a crypto token, and the company and its investors hold a lot of WLD too. The longer-term plan is to charge businesses and apps for verifying real humans, and to sell Orbs and services.

The pushback is real: scanning eyeballs in exchange for crypto set off privacy alarms, and regulators in several countries have suspended or investigated it.

Blania came on TBPN to talk about fighting deepfakes and AI fraud.

Private. The Orb sees you. Hopefully the bots can't.

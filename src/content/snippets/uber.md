For years the big fear was that robotaxis would kill Uber.

Uber's answer: fine, we'll just be the app you hail the robots from.

The quick history: founded in 2009 by Travis Kalanick and Garrett Camp as UberCab, a way to summon a black car from your phone. Then it ate the taxi industry, fought regulators in every city on Earth, spawned Uber Eats, and lost billions of dollars for about a decade straight before becoming solidly profitable.

How it makes money: Uber takes a cut of every ride and delivery. In the April–June 2026 quarter, people booked more than \$58 billion worth of trips and orders through Uber, up 22%.

The part most people don't know: **Uber is an advertising company now.** Those "sponsored" restaurants at the top of Uber Eats and the ads you see while waiting for your ride add up to well over \$2 billion a year, growing more than 50%. You are a captive audience in the back seat.

And the robots: rather than building its own self-driving cars (it tried, then sold that unit), Uber partners with everyone who does. It has more than 30 autonomous vehicle partners and plans robotaxi trips in up to 15 cities by the end of 2026. Whoever wins self-driving, the plan is that they still need Uber's 200 million monthly users.

It's also an investor in Zipline and is helping push drone deliveries.

Public (NYSE: UBER). CEO Dara Khosrowshahi. Five stars, would ride again. Even with nobody up front.

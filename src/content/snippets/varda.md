Varda makes drugs in space.

Legal ones. Pharmaceuticals. Please put the Breaking Bad RV jokes away. Actually, keep one handy.

Here's the science: in zero gravity, crystals form differently. Some medicines can only be made in certain crystal structures that are hard or impossible to produce on Earth, and those structures can change how well a drug works or how long it stays stable. Varda's first mission in 2023 made a version of the HIV drug ritonavir in a crystal form that's tough to get on the ground.

How it works: a capsule about the size of a dishwasher rides into orbit on a SpaceX rocket, runs its chemistry up there for weeks, then falls back to Earth through the atmosphere at hypersonic speed and parachutes down in the desert, so far in Utah and South Australia.

The early drama: its first capsule was ready to come home in 2023 but got stuck in orbit for months waiting on government permission to land. Varda ended up in a very public fight with regulators over a capsule full of space medicine circling overhead.

How it makes money right now, the part most people don't know: **mostly defense.** Every time Varda's capsule screams back through the atmosphere, it's a rare chance to collect real hypersonic flight data, which the military badly wants for missile research. So the same capsule can carry drugs for pharma companies and sensors for the Pentagon.

Founded in 2021 by Will Bruey, an ex-SpaceX engineer, and Delian Asparouhov of Founders Fund. Six missions so far, with seven more planned for 2027 alone.

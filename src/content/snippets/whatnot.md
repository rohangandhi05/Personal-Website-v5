Your parents' generation had QVC: a host on TV holding up a necklace, a phone number, "only 12 left!"

Your generation has Whatnot. Same energy, different universe.

On Whatnot, sellers go live on video and auction things in real time: Pokémon cards, sports cards, sneakers, vintage clothes, Funko Pops, even live plants. Buyers watch, chat and bid in seconds-long auctions. The single most electric thing on the app is the "rip": a seller opens a fresh pack of cards on stream, and everyone watches to see if a rare one comes out. It's part shopping, part game show, part Twitch.

It was started in 2019 by Grant LaFontaine and Logan Head. Founders say they got turned down by around 100 investors early on. Pokémon cards were the first big category, and trading and sports cards are still the top sellers.

How it makes money: a commission on every sale, around 8%, plus a payment processing fee. The more people buy, the more Whatnot earns. And people buy a lot: about \$8 billion of stuff was sold through live auctions on Whatnot in 2025, more than double the year before, and it passed another \$8 billion in just the first half of 2026.

The thing most people don't realize: live shopping was supposed to be a fad that never took off in the U.S. Whatnot proved the opposite, and it's now competing with eBay and TikTok Shop.

In August 2026 it raised \$545 million at a \$20 billion valuation, nearly double its valuation from ten months earlier. CEO Grant LaFontaine has been on TBPN.

Private. Gotta sell 'em all.

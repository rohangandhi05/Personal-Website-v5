WHOOP has no screen. No clock. No notifications. It's a strap on your wrist that silently watches your heart, sleep and strain, and tells you on your phone how "recovered" you are each morning. Usually: not very.

Here's the business model most people find surprising: **you don't buy the band.** You pay for a membership, and the band comes with it. WHOOP isn't a hardware company, it's a subscription company that happens to ship hardware. That means steady, recurring revenue, the thing investors love most.

It was founded in 2012 by Will Ahmed, who captained the squash team at Harvard and kept overtraining. He wanted a device that would tell athletes when to push and when to rest. Pro athletes became the first evangelists. Cristiano Ronaldo and LeBron James are now investors.

The growth: in 2026 WHOOP raised \$575 million at a \$10.1 billion valuation, with Abbott, the medical device giant, among the investors. It has talked publicly about reaching \$1 billion in annual sales.

The plan is to go from fitness tracker to health platform. Its Advanced Labs service lets members get blood tests, from \$199, that combine with their WHOOP data and AI coaching.

The fight: in July 2025 the FDA sent WHOOP a warning letter saying its Blood Pressure Insights feature was acting like an unapproved medical device. WHOOP argued it's a wellness feature, not a diagnosis. It's the central tension of the whole category: how much "health" can a wellness gadget talk about before it becomes medicine?
